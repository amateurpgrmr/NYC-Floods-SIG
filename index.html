Flood Zone Definitions

Zone AE: Areas with a 1% annual chance of flooding (100-year floodplain). Base Flood Elevations are determined.

Zone VE: Coastal areas with 1% or greater annual chance of flooding with additional hazards from storm-induced velocity wave action.

October 30, 2025 - Historic Flooding Timeline

Pre-Event (Oct 29): NYC Emergency Management issued warnings for heavy rain expected to last 8+ hours.

Morning (6-10 AM): Rainfall began across all five boroughs. Initial accumulation within normal range.

Midday (10 AM-2 PM): Rainfall intensity dramatically increased. Peak rate: 1 inch in 10 minutes (6 inches/hour) vs. system capacity of 1.75 inches/hour.

Central Park: 1.85 inches total, breaking 108-year record (1917: 1.64 inches).

LaGuardia Airport: 2.09 inches, surpassing 1955 record (1.18 inches).

Newark Airport: 1.99 inches, beating 1955 record (1.57 inches).

Critical Period (12-3 PM): Widespread street flooding, subway disruptions citywide. Two weeks of rain fell in hours.

Fatalities: Two men trapped and died in flooded basements - one in Crown Heights, Brooklyn; one in Washington Heights, Manhattan.

Infrastructure Impact: Sewer system overwhelmed, submerged vehicles across Queens and Brooklyn, subway delays on all lines.

Aftermath: Event highlighted vulnerabilities in aging infrastructure and need for climate adaptation measures.

Data Layers

USGS River Gauges (15 stations): Real-time monitoring of gage height across NYC waterways including Bronx River, Alley Creek, and Valley Stream.

Hurricane Evacuation Centers (60 facilities): Official NYC emergency processing centers where residents are staged before transport to shelters.

Sentinel Satellites (8 areas): Recent flood detections from ESA Copernicus Sentinel-1 and Sentinel-2 with 84-95% confidence levels.

Stormwater Infrastructure (10 facilities): Green infrastructure including rain gardens, bioswales, and detention basins with 200,000+ gallons total capacity.

$19.5B

Economic Damage from Hurricane Sandy (2012)

NYC's costliest flood disaster destroyed 650,000 homes, devastated Lower Manhattan's financial district, and caused $19 billion in damages across the five boroughs. Storm surge reached 14 feet in some areas.

Source: NYC Mayor's Office, FEMA.

400,000+

Residents Living in Flood Zones

Over 400,000 New Yorkers live in FEMA-designated flood zones, with 71,500 buildings at risk. Brooklyn and Queens contain the highest concentration of vulnerable properties, particularly in coastal neighborhoods like Red Hook and Rockaway.

Source: NYC Emergency Management, FEMA.

43 Deaths

Hurricane Sandy Fatalities in NYC

43 New York City residents lost their lives during Hurricane Sandy, with many drowning in flooded basements or from storm-related incidents. The October 2025 floods added 2 more fatalities, highlighting ongoing basement flooding dangers.

Source: NYC Health Department, NYC Emergency Management.

520%

Increase in Extreme Rain Events Since 1950s

NYC has experienced a 520% increase in extreme precipitation events (rainfall exceeding 2 inches in 24 hours) compared to the 1950s baseline. Climate change is accelerating this trend, overwhelming aging infrastructure designed for historical rainfall patterns.

Source: NOAA National Weather Service, NYC Climate Panel.

2-6 Feet

Projected Sea Level Rise by 2100

NYC faces 2-6 feet of sea level rise by 2100, threatening subway systems, airports, hospitals, and power infrastructure. A 6-foot rise would inundate 37% of Lower Manhattan and displace hundreds of thousands of residents citywide.

Source: NYC Panel on Climate Change, NOAA.

$20B+

Investment Needed for Climate Resilience

NYC requires over $20 billion in flood protection infrastructure including coastal barriers, elevated buildings, upgraded sewers, and green infrastructure. Current projects include the $1.45B East Side Coastal Resiliency and $615M South Battery Park City Resiliency.

Source: NYC Mayor's Office of Climate Resiliency.
